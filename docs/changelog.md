# Literature Feed: changelog

## Next version (not released)
- Commands are grouped by what you are doing and named that way in the command palette (**Watch:**, **Discover:**, **Expand:**, **Improve:**, **Tools:**). The telescope ribbon icon is now the **Find papers** menu with the same groups. The commands **Open review graph** and **Open the graph in this note** are gone: the menu opens the last graph and **Open this note's report view** opens a note's graph. The ids of the other commands are unchanged.

- Each search has a **Seed collection** (default: its Zotero collection followed by `/seeds`) and **Known papers (extra DOIs)**. In the review, **Fill from seed collection** puts a search's known papers in the **Papers I already know** box, with the counts and the cost shown first, and says what is wrong when the collection is missing or empty or when items have no DOI. The Zotero index now stores which collections each item is in and leaves out items in the Trash (an older index is read once more in full). The test of suggested searches checks at most 40 papers.
- Check setup has a row for each search's seed collection (ok, not set, the same as the inbox or project collection, or not found with near matches). **Create it** makes a missing one in Zotero when you press it (last name only, the parent must exist). If Zotero cannot be read, one row says so. In the review, **Find DOIs for these** sends the titles of seed items that have no DOI to the Find DOIs tab. A fix of an unknown kind no longer sets the literature folder.
- The review's papers table is now the report table of the other tools: chips (rank, search, in Zotero or not, and with known papers: known paper, fits, doesn't fit), a search box, sortable columns, **Copy as CSV** and, with known papers, **Overlap** and **Fit** columns. It opens in the order the prompt, the note and the graph use, so what you see is what they take.
- In the review, after **Read papers and suggest searches**, a **How your papers link** box lists papers linked to almost none of the others (they may not belong, or the search needs a second strand) and the strands the rest fall into, named by shared phrases. Profiles saved before this ask you to press the button again.
- In the review, tick papers in the new **Add** column and press **Add N to this week's inbox**: they join the chosen search's list in this week's reading-inbox note (the note is made if it is not there), papers already listed for that search are left out and named, and each ticked paper shows its fit to the known papers. Sync then sends them to Zotero as usual; the item's Extra line says "Found by: OpenAlex review" instead of "search". Added lines carry a hidden `%%via:review%%` marker.
- **Promote to seeds**: in the review table (a **Seed** column, for papers already in Zotero) and in the reading-inbox table (a link on sent papers). It asks first, then adds the paper's Zotero item to the search's seed collection, so it counts as a known paper; **Undo** in the message takes it out again. In the review the question says how the paper fits the seeds and how their search strings would change; in the inbox it says the fit was not checked. Only the item's collection changes in Zotero.
- **Improve tab** for each search (the **Improve** button on its inbox card, the sliders icon on its settings row, **Improve a search** in the Find papers menu and the command palette): how the search is doing (wanted rate per search string, with the weak-evidence note under 20 decided papers), its overlap with the other searches, how its known papers link, search strings built from the known papers (what each finds, leave-one-out, size check, **Add to this search**) and buttons for the LLM prompts. **Show search effectiveness** still opens the old report.
- **Library map** (Find papers → Improve → Library map, the command, or the button in the Improve tab): reads your Zotero library's papers from OpenAlex (the price, about $0.0001 per 50, is on the button), groups papers that cite the same works or share authors, names each group from the phrases it shares, lists outliers and papers OpenAlex had no record of. You can rename a group, merge or split groups, move papers and say which search a group is for. Nothing is written to Zotero yet, and the groups are not saved when the tab closes.
- Citation gaps: a **Review?** column marks works that are probably reviews (OpenAlex type, a review journal, 150 or more references, or a review-like title; the reason is in the tooltip), a **Reviews** chip filters them, and a **Niche** column (cited-by count per citation, tooltip gives the percentile) sorts by how few others cite a work. Pointers only: nothing is reordered or scored.
- Citation gaps: **Also find similar papers** (off by default). It reads the works that cite the paper's references, finds the ones that share at least five of them, and lists what the 30 most similar of those cite and the paper does not. The real price and the references it will skip (cited 2000 times or more) are shown first, and you can skip the scan. The table gets **Both routes** and **From similar papers** chips and **Similar papers** and **Gap** columns; the note gets a "Cited by similar papers" section and records the scan so Repeat re-runs it. Pointers only, with the thresholds shown beside the list length.
- Citation gaps: with the similar-paper scan on, a **Chart** tab beside Works and Authors plots each work the similar papers cite by the share of your references that cite it (across) and the share of the similar papers that cite it (up), with a dashed equal-share line, a ring on works found on both routes, the five largest gaps labelled, and a click opening the work's details. Works cited only by the similar papers now show how many of your references really cite them (it showed 0).
- References: pressing **Tidy and find DOIs** again keeps what you did to lines that have not changed: a DOI you typed, Confirm, Skip and the tick, and does not ask Crossref about them again. A line you edit (apart from spacing and capital letters) is looked up as new, and a lookup that failed is tried again. A note says how many were kept.

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-13)
- Reports share one look: tables with chips, sorting and a search box, a side pane for details, and **Open this note's report view** reopens a report from its note.
- Citation gaps: one table of works and one of authors, switched by chips; clicking an author opens their papers beside the table (it did nothing before for runs saved by an older version); the settings fold away after a run; the gaps note can reopen the view; an LLM prompt checks whether under- or over-cited authors really are, against what it finds on the web, and its verdicts come back into the table and the note.
- Literature review: **Works fetched per topic** (default 1000, up to 2000; it was 300) and **Order papers by**: closest to the papers you already know, best fit to the searches, newest or most cited (citation order used to push recent, well-fitting papers out of the prompt).
- Literature review: **Papers I already know** (paste DOIs) ranks results by shared references and authors, adds that overlap to the prompt, and **Read papers and suggest searches** proposes search strings from those papers, shows which of them each string finds, checks its size and adds it to a search.

### Also in 0.3.0 (built earlier on this branch)
- The references screen matches Check setup: marks, confirm, skip, links to the DOI, Google and Scholar, selectable text. Every list of papers shows its authors. In the gaps tool an author can be clicked for their cited and uncited papers and for their other work on the same ground; the author table can be searched; the gaps note carries all author tables.
- Citation gaps: add references by DOI (they count as cited and are read), and an authors table: for each author the share of the paper's references and the share of the gaps they appear on, the difference in points, labels for under- and over-cited authors, the paper's own authors starred; in the tab (with CSV) and in the gaps note.
- Citation gaps: up to 100 gaps with Show more, and an optional second level (what the works that several references cite rest on), with the cost shown first (about $0.0004 a paper, about $0.0011 with the second level).
- Find DOIs for a reference list: paste references copied from a PDF (ragged lines, no numbers or DOIs), and each is split out and looked up on Crossref (new host `api.crossref.org`, only when you press the button; optional contact email in the settings). Results are marked Found, Check, Not found, Manual or Error, the ones that need a look come first and are skipped unless ticked, and you can copy the list with DOIs or as CSV, or make a note. The citation-gaps paste mode uses it, so pasted references now cost about $0.0001 per 50 instead of $0.001 each.
- Review graph in a review note: the same chips, legend and details as the tab (hold Ctrl or Cmd to zoom, so scrolling the note still works), and an Open in a tab chip. Pointing at a paper names only its ten most cited neighbours.
- The canvas is now a reading board instead of a copy of the network: a labelled group per theme (or per group of papers that cite each other), a readable card per paper with its link, citations, what you have and the LLM's advice, colours by reading level, and one line per paper to the most cited paper in the set that it cites.
- Review graph: chips instead of a drop-down, each explained; a **Timeline** layout with the year on the vertical axis; groups laid out on their own so small ones sit beside the main one; papers with no link hidden at first; the view fits the window; older saved graphs get the current layout when opened.
- Review graph: the 12 most cited papers are always labelled, pointing at a paper lights its neighbours, big groups carry a name, a new colour mode by the LLM's themes, bigger papers, zoom buttons and a legend.
- Citation gaps of one paper as its own tab: a DOI, link or OpenAlex id, an item picked from your Zotero library, or a pasted reference list; the cost is shown first (about $0.0003 for a paper, $0.001 per pasted reference without a DOI); a note records how it was made.
- Literature review graph: papers are drawn again (they were missing from the graph tab, so its controls did nothing), and papers with no link to another sit in a row along the bottom instead of against the walls.
- Literature review: the note records how the review was made (**How this review was made**: topics as written, matches found, ranking rules, window and sort, first request of each topic, LLM named, prompt hash and the prompt kept as a note, the answer as pasted, which paper was P1, P2 …). The command **Repeat the review in this note** runs it again with the recorded or the current search definitions and shows what changed.
- Literature review: the review opens in a **tab** instead of a pop-up and is **saved as you go** (`review-session.json`): clicking elsewhere, closing the tab or restarting Obsidian no longer loses it, and the tab offers **Resume** or **Start new**. A telescope icon in the ribbon opens a menu (new review, resume the last one, open the last graph); the command **Resume the last literature review** does the same.
- Literature review: **Open graph** shows the citation network in a tab (own SVG, no library: pan, zoom, click for details; colour by group, citing groups, year or the LLM's reading level; a ring for papers in Zotero, a dot for papers in the vault), and the command **Open review graph** reopens the last one. The graph is also drawn inside the review note (a `literature-feed-graph` block with an **Open full graph** button), the Open graph button is at the top of the review window, and there is a ribbon icon and a command **Open the graph in this note**.
- Literature review: charts (papers per year, frequent authors, most cited, most connected), the citation network inside the set at no extra cost, **Find missing papers** (works the set cites often, and works citing several of its papers, flagged against Zotero and the vault, within the spending limit), **Find gaps** for one paper (what many of its references cite that it does not), **Save canvas** (the network as an Obsidian canvas), and a `literature-feed-chart` code block that draws a chart in a note.
- Literature review: **Generate literature review** runs your searches over a wider window within a spending limit (estimate first, cap per topic, stop at the limit, 24-hour result cache, a monthly tally of the plugin's OpenAlex requests with an optional soft limit), flags papers already in Zotero (a local read-only index of the library), already in the vault and listed before, builds a prompt for an LLM to group and prioritise them, and makes a review note from the pasted answer. Settings → Literature review has the limits.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-13)
LLM prompts you copy and paste, a Check setup window, overlap between searches, and a tidier reading inbox. Desktop only, as 0.1.x.
- The Reading inbox tile on a Home note or hub is a `[!reading-inbox]` callout: a book icon and a title that is a link (`obsidian://literature-feed-inbox`) opening the newest weekly note; Check setup's integration step writes it that way, with no marker comments in your notes (a tile is found again by its title link, the Vault spec section by its heading), and it does not offer a second tile where you already made a reading inbox card. The tile's title looks like Lab Kit's other tile titles (plain text, underlined on hover). The same link works anywhere in a note.
- Overlap: the search report gets an Overlap section (papers two searches both list, papers one search's rules dropped that another lists, topics that add nothing beyond another, with plain sentences), the table says "also in <search>" for a paper listed under several searches, the tweak prompt carries the topic overlap, and a new prompt **Compare searches with an LLM** asks for advice on keeping, narrowing, merging or splitting searches (shown to read, never applied). The history now keeps the ids of papers a search's rules dropped (ids only, up to 4000 per search).
- Check setup now adds its explanation to Lab Kit's How my vault works (a callout at the end of the Reading papers section, marked as an addition from the plugin) instead of the Vault spec, and offers a stand-alone Reading inbox how-to note when Lab Kit is not there. The Accept and Reject buttons in the LLM review window turn green and red.
- Check setup: a command (and the first row of the settings) that lists what the plugin needs, with a fix button where it can: keys, Zotero user ID, inbox, archive and literature folders, a search switched on, each search's project against a note of that name, ZotLit, Lab Kit. When Lab Kit is on, a last step offers to add the Reading inbox card to the Home note and the Literature hub and a Reading inbox section to the Vault spec, each only on its own **Add** after a preview, and removable.
- LLM prompts by copy and paste: **Review a search with an LLM**, **Tweak search terms with an LLM** and **New search from a description with an LLM** (commands and Settings → LLM prompts). Each copies a prompt that states the JSON format the answer must have, and takes the pasted answer back; suggestions are accepted or rejected one by one, and decisions are remembered in `decisions.json` for the next review. The plugin sends nothing and has no LLM key.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-13)
First release. Desktop only (Windows, macOS, Linux); tried so far on Windows, and not every feature has been checked yet. Weekly OpenAlex feeds into a reading-inbox note with a table and per-search cards, Zotero sync, read state and archive, dismissing papers, a search-effectiveness report, and an automatic weekly scan. You need an OpenAlex key and, for sync, a Zotero key; see the README.

- Schedule: the plugin scans by itself when Obsidian starts (about 15 seconds after it is ready, then hourly while it stays open) once the scan day has passed since the last scan, if a feed is switched on (setting **Scan on start-up when due**, on by default); an optional once-a-day Sync of the ticked papers (setting, off by default). Automatic runs do not open the note and stay silent when there is nothing to do. New command **Open reading inbox** and a ribbon icon open the newest weekly note.
- Dismissing a paper at any stage: a **✕** on each table row with **Not relevant** and **Can't get it (no access)**, a new status Dismissed (also for papers already sent to Zotero or read), an undo, a Dismissed filter chip and count; a dismissed paper counts as done for the archive at once and is never sent. The tick box of a paper in Zotero is locked, and **Send again** / **Cancel resend** in its Links cell replace the untick-to-resend trick. The search report treats "not relevant" as a firm no and shows "no access" papers (counted as wanted) in their own column. The demo set has a dismissed example of each.
- Table: a **Sync N to Zotero** button at the right end of the filter chips, right above the table (N = papers it would send; greyed when none), ticked rows are tinted across in their status colour, tick boxes are larger and blue, and the table cells are centred vertically.
- The table has a Year column (its header was there but the cells were missing, so the headers sat one column off); the cards keep their Show in table button at the bottom.
- Search effectiveness: the plugin keeps a local history (`ledger.json` in its folder) of every listed paper and what you did with it (ticked, sent to Zotero, read), of what each search topic returned and had removed in each scan, and of changes to a search's rules; the command **Show search effectiveness** shows, per search, wanted and dismissed papers by tier, topic, month and rule version, with feed terms and title words that do better or worse than average as suggestions, and can copy the report as Markdown.
- `npm run demo` writes a demo set into `test-vault/Demo/` (four weekly notes, literature notes, a guide) and `docs/feeds/` has two demo searches to import.
- Cards per search: above the table the note shows one card for each search with a badge ("3 to read"), topic count and last-scan numbers, the first papers to read, the status counts, a funnel line (listed, ticked, read) with a status bar, and **Show in table**, which filters the table to that search (a "Feed" chip clears it). The Home card (`show: home`) gains the status counts and the first papers to read (`limit: N`, default 3, at most 20). The per-search rows of the status box now appear on the Home card only.
- Reading inbox table: the block shows every paper in a table with status chips (New, Ticked, In Zotero, Read, Processed) in Lab Kit's colours by meaning, an order that puts papers to read first, sort by clicking a column header, filter chips with counts, and tick boxes that edit the note's lines; the per-feed lists are hidden in reading view by the class `literature-feed-table`. Notes now carry `cssclasses` `vt`, `literature-feed`, `literature-feed-table` (old notes get them on the next refresh). The "In Zotero" callout is amber.
- Archive rule: a weekly note moves to the archive when every paper is read or has aged out (unticked, or in Zotero but unread, N weeks after the note's week); papers in Zotero that are not read keep it in the inbox as a reading queue. Setting renamed "Mark papers done after (weeks)".
- Where papers came from: new Zotero items get the tag `found-by/openalex` and an Extra line "Found by: OpenAlex search, feed ..., topic N"; inbox lines carry a hidden marker with the topics that found the paper. Items already in the library are not tagged.
- Read state: a paper also matches a literature note by its title (a `title` property) when the key and DOI do not match; the command **Update read state and archive**, run by hand, says what it checked and changed, and says so when the inbox folder holds no inbox notes.
- Read state and archive: a paper with a literature note (found by DOI or Zotero key in any frontmatter property of the notes in the literature folder) gets a `Read` link; a finished weekly note (every paper in Zotero, read or dismissed after N weeks) moves to the archive folder; new settings for the literature folder, the archive switch and the dismiss weeks; command **Update read state and archive**.
- README: a section "Needed for PDFs" (Zotero desktop, the Zotero Connector, and the Zotero add-on Better Find Full Text); Sync adds metadata only.
- Zotero sync, checks and resync: Sync reads each item back and counts it as synced only if it is in the library and in the right collections; synced papers move into an "In Zotero" list per feed, and unticking one there sends it again; the Notice says which collections the papers were filed in; trashed collections count as missing; the command **Show Zotero collections** lists what Zotero's server reports; Sync saves an open note before reading it.
- Zotero sync: the command **Sync ticked papers** and the status block's **Sync** button send every ticked paper to your Zotero library: a new item (journal article, preprint or thesis) in the feed's collection and project collection with the tag `feed/<name>`, or, when the DOI is already there, the collections and tag added to the existing item. The note line gets an "in Zotero" link. Collections are never created unless two new settings allow it (inbox and project separately, off by default; only the last name of a path); a missing one stops the sync with a message. The feed editor now starts with name, start year, Zotero collection and project. First real sync worked.
- Reading inbox: the command **Scan now** searches OpenAlex for every switched-on feed and writes or extends this week's note (`<year>-W<week> Reading inbox.md`): a status block with **Scan now** and a disabled **Sync**, one collapsible callout per feed, one checkbox line per paper, a separate list for papers with no abstract. Ticks survive a rescan; papers already listed are not listed again. A `show: home` block gives a small card for another note (flat inside a callout such as a Home column, in Lab Kit's card colours when that theme is on); a paper found by two feeds counts once in the "waiting" figures; buttons are outlined pills like Lab Kit's; a line says what the latest scan found, including how many papers the feed rules dropped; with more than one feed switched on, a row per feed shows the same.
- API keys are kept in Obsidian's secret storage, not in the vault; the settings file holds only the secret's name. Keys saved as plain text by an earlier build are moved on first load.
- Settings tab: OpenAlex and Zotero keys, Zotero user ID, inbox and archive folders, scan day; a feed list (switch on or off, edit, duplicate, delete, export and import as `.json`); a feed editor (topics, concept groups with roles, drop and lower terms, years from, Zotero collection, project) with warnings and a **Test** button that runs a real OpenAlex search and shows how the papers would score. Settings are read tolerantly and reload when `data.json` changes outside Obsidian.
- First feed as an importable file: `docs/feeds/lipoic-acid-copolymers.json`.
- Matching: a paper with no abstract whose title does not show the required terms is "Unchecked" (to be listed apart in the inbox), not Dropped.
- Matching: a paper with no abstract is scored on its title alone and ranks at most Possible. In the inbox note such a paper carries "title only".
- Repo scaffold: a plugin that loads and unloads, build, test and lint set up, CI workflows. No feature yet.
